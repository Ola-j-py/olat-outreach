Executive summary

Lead with the object, not a broad colour range.

Beam Paints combines handmade watercolour, high pigment content, a distinctive Paintstone format and plastic-free presentation. In Japan, the clearest first step is a tightly edited assortment that retailers can explain and demonstrate in minutes—before considering wider distribution.

Entry concept

A three-SKU “Portable Colour Stories” pilot.

Key questions to validate

Answer these before approaching retailers.

Wholesale economics

MOQ, lead time, export carton size, recommended retail pricing and any territory expectations for Japan.

Product documentation

Current SKU-level ingredient/CAS data, SDS coverage and the exact language permitted for safety claims.

Story permissions

Approved Japanese translations for brand, material and cultural context—including product names that should remain untranslated.

Pilot decision rule

What level of retailer interest, sample requests or first-order volume would justify a second test.

Recommended validation

A compact path from evidence to first order.

Phase 01

Confirm the offer

Select three SKUs, wholesale terms and the approved product story for Japan.

Phase 02

Clear the basics

Confirm classification, ingredients, safety copy, importer details and landed-cost assumptions.

Phase 03

Test six buyers

Approach a narrow mix of art-supply, museum/design and craft-led stores with samples and a one-page sell sheet.

Phase 04

Run one micro-drop

Use a swatch demo or short workshop to measure conversion, product questions and repeat interest.

Launch design

Keep the first test precise and respectful.

Assortment

Use a simple entry/core/gift price ladder. Avoid introducing the full colour system before the retailer understands what customers respond to.

Merchandising

Include one swatch surface, one process card and a compact explanation of materials. Let the product demonstrate the claim.

Localization

Use brand-approved Japanese copy. Preserve names and cultural context where translation would flatten meaning or create misrepresentation.

Regulatory considerations

Confirm HS classification, screen ingredient/CAS information against Japanese chemical rules, and prepare Japanese use/safety information with the local importer before sale.
