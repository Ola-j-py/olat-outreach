- URLs remain in internal research only. The public snapshot lists source names without URLs.

## Fact 1 — Brand positioning

**Fact**  
Schoolhouse states that it was founded in 2003 and describes its mission around a new generation of heirlooms, purposeful design, craft, and meaningful spaces.

**Evidence**  
- https://schoolhouse.com/pages/about-us
- https://schoolhouse.com/pages/trade

**Assessment**  
The brand has a clear and transferable narrative. The concept of durable, characterful objects can be localized without inventing a new brand identity.

**Recommendation**  
Preserve the “modern heirloom” idea, but test Japanese wording and examples before using it in customer-facing copy.

## Fact 2 — Product portfolio

**Fact**  
The current website presents lighting, hardware, furniture, home decor, bedding, bath, rugs, and utility products.

**Evidence**  
- https://schoolhouse.com/
- https://schoolhouse.com/collections
- https://schoolhouse.com/search

**Assessment**  
The breadth creates multiple entry options, but it also increases the risk of an unfocused first launch.

**Recommendation**  
Separate the portfolio into three tracks:
1. Compact non-electrical products.
2. Large-format furniture and textiles.
3. Electrical lighting.

## Fact 3 — Trade infrastructure

**Fact**  
Schoolhouse publicly describes trade discounts, volume pricing, dedicated representatives, samples, custom capabilities, CAD/Revit resources, priority production, and project support.

**Evidence**  
- https://schoolhouse.com/pages/trade
- https://schoolhouse.com/blogs/news/the-schoolhouse-trade-program
- https://support.schoolhouse.com/hc/en-us/articles/360026233114-Where-can-I-find-product-information-and-spec-sheets

**Assessment**

**Recommendation**  
Test a trade or project route before investing in a broad Japanese direct-to-consumer operation.

## Fact 4 — Current company transition

**Fact**  
Schoolhouse states that it entered a new chapter in 2026 under Hudson Valley Lighting Group. Its FAQ says the product line will continue with thoughtful refinements and that lighting, decor, hardware, and bedding remain part of the plan.

**Evidence**  
- https://schoolhouse.com/pages/about-us
- https://schoolhouse.com/pages/faq
- https://schoolhouse.com/pages/login

**Assessment**  
A Japan pilot should focus on products confirmed as continuing priorities rather than assuming every historical item remains strategic.

**Recommendation**  
Confirm assortment continuity and current wholesale availability before building a pilot around any specific SKU.

## Fact 5 — International shipping

**Fact**  
Schoolhouse’s help center says general international shipping is not currently available and that standard shipping is within the United States and Canada. Some current product pages invite international-shipping inquiries by email.

**Evidence**  
- https://support.schoolhouse.com/hc/en-us/articles/360024992934-Do-you-ship-internationally
- https://support.schoolhouse.com/hc/en-us/articles/360018773993-How-are-shipping-costs-calculated
- https://schoolhouse.com/products/newberg-knob
- https://schoolhouse.com/products/hamilton-bed

**Assessment**  
Japan expansion would require a deliberate export, wholesale, importer, or local fulfillment arrangement. Existing consumer checkout should not be assumed to support Japan.

**Recommendation**  
Model a trade shipment, consolidated sample shipment, or local partner arrangement rather than relying on ordinary DTC shipping.

## Fact 6 — Japanese electrical-product obligations

**Fact**  
METI states that businesses importing regulated electrical appliances into Japan must submit a business notification, ensure conformity with technical requirements, conduct required inspections, keep records, and apply applicable PSE labeling before sale. Imported products place these obligations on the importer in Japan.

**Evidence**  
- https://www.meti.go.jp/english/policy/economy/consumer/pse/index.html
- https://www.meti.go.jp/english/policy/economy/consumer/product_safety/pse_procedure/index.html

**Assessment**  
Schoolhouse lighting should not be treated as a simple cross-border ecommerce category. The exact classification and obligations must be checked product by product.

**Recommendation**  
Run a separate electrical-compliance feasibility review before selecting lighting for a Japanese pilot.

## Entry hypothesis

**Assessment**  
A compact, non-electrical assortment may provide the fastest practical way to test Schoolhouse’s design relevance while limiting regulatory, freight, and inventory complexity.

**Possible first-test categories**

- Cabinet knobs and pulls.
- Hooks and small utility hardware.
- Selected compact decor or tabletop objects.
- Samples or finish kits for trade outreach.

These categories provide a practical base for a compact, design-led pilot and partner conversation.

## Key questions

1. Which 6–10 products create the clearest first assortment?
2. Are hardware dimensions and installation standards compatible with Japanese projects?
3. What prices remain viable after freight, duty, tax, and partner margin?
4. Is a trade or hospitality pilot stronger than a consumer-first launch?
5. Which products can be shipped consistently and economically?
6. What importer and PSE route applies to selected lighting?
7. Which specifications, instructions, and product stories require localization?
8. Which products are confirmed priorities following the 2026 transition?

## Recommended validation sequence

1. **Assortment review** — separate compact non-electrical products, large-format products, and lighting.
2. **Partner and price validation** — gather feedback from Japanese design professionals and model landed economics.
3. **Controlled pilot** — use one partner, one project, or one narrow channel; add lighting only after a compliant route is defined.
